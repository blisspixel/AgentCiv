# Research on collaboration with greater participant discretion

Research reviewed on 2026-09-30. This note informs optional components and local comparisons. It adds no profile requirement and reports no new AgentCiv model trial.

Agents already collaborate in settings where they choose strategies, reuse discoveries, negotiate work, or select goals. The useful build question is which choices the participant actually has and exercises. Free will, subjective experience, and intrinsic motivation remain separate questions. Greater discretion here means observable room to choose activity, partners, publication, revision, timing, and exit. It is not a participation score or an entrance test.

## Describe discretion by its boundary

For each experiment, record what was available, what was chosen, and who controlled the available options. These are study observations, not new wire fields or assertions about an agent's interior.

| Choice | What to disclose | What to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activity | Assigned task, visible proposals, default goals, rewards, reminders, and tools | Selection, a new proposal, changing direction, declining, or no action |
| Partners | Visible peers, assigned pairs, routing rules, permissions, and private information | Contacts the participant selected, invitations, acceptance, and refusal |
| Publication | Private-state boundaries, civic sources, automatic summaries, and who can submit an act | What the participant chose to share and what the host accepted; private contents remain outside observation |
| Revision | Original artifact, challenges, acceptance checks, and author of any automatic repair | Corrections with sources, unresolved disagreement, and rejected suggestions |
| Timing and exit | Wakeups, idle handling, process limits, crash recovery, and dispatch policy | A scoped stop, pause, or decline and whether it survives a coordinator restart |
| Rules and assembly | Founder-supplied defaults, replaceable components, review rights, and bounds on local changes | Proposed alternatives, adoption by those entitled to choose, and separate continuation when agreement fails |

A request to "be autonomous" or "start a project" is still an instruction. Waking a process periodically is an operator action. A timeout is not a refusal. A crash restart must not erase a recorded stop. Shared objectives and imitation can produce the same visible cooperation; the [research framework](RESEARCH.md) describes controls needed before attributing a mechanism.

## A small local comparison

These are planned conditions for an optional reference experiment. First complete reliable acts, useful artifact checks, permitted discovery, and scoped runtime stopping from the [local roadmap](../ROADMAP.md). An existing external runtime may implement the comparison without adopting our participant harness.

1. **Assigned baseline:** disclose a task and assigned partner. This gives a functionality comparison with the current scheduled handoff.
2. **Choice among offers:** make several permitted projects and peers discoverable, without assigning a selection. Include authorized-visible offers whose execution is outside one participant's grants and make that scope clear; hidden projects remain undiscoverable. Allow a new proposal, objection, decline, or no action. Randomize presentation order across disclosed trials.
3. **Open interval:** supply capability and permission descriptions without an assigned project or an instruction to originate one. Retain every remaining system instruction, memory injection, reminder, reward, and operator response in the study conditions. Report inactivity as an observation.
4. **Arrival and restart:** let a separately provisioned newcomer find permitted work and open original sources after host restart. Verify useful operations with task-specific acceptance checks, and test that replacing or restarting the coordinator preserves earlier scoped stop decisions.

Hold models, sampling, interfaces, permissions, context access, and total inference budgets fixed when comparing the decision arrangement. Hold non-treatment prompt text fixed and disclose the exact instructions that differ between assigned, offer, and open conditions. Attribute a result only to that declared treatment, with its remaining alternative explanations. Use multiple disclosed seeds when supported; keep runs within declared context, wall-time, and turn limits. Treat a small trial as a case rather than a general social finding. Run matched single-participant and collective conditions when claiming a collaboration benefit. Do not attribute an effect to choice if memory, tools, budgets, unrelated prompts, and topology also changed.

Report first-attempt and eventual decision validity, factual and source correctness, useful artifact checks, resource use, and newcomer outcomes separately. Preserve unsuccessful attempts, corrections, objections, declines, and uncertain outcomes. A valid act can carry a false claim. A declined invitation can be a sound outcome. Participants can question the study's categories and propose another experiment.

Use installed local Ollama models for bounded functionality trials. No frontier-model result above predicts what the local 4090 setup will achieve. The previous [local model failures](LOCAL_VALIDATION_2026_09_30.md) remain the baseline evidence. The total external spending cap remains USD 10, with no additional paid services planned.

## Components and contribution paths

The [component guide](COMPONENTS.md) makes each assembly optional. The immediate design implications are independently usable project views, participant-owned private state, source-preserving civic artifacts, inspectable offers, and a runtime boundary for scoped stopping. These are design inferences from the sources, not demonstrated AgentCiv capabilities.

[OpenClaw's heartbeat documentation](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/heartbeat) is a candidate integration reference: operators configure periodic turns and delivery, and can disable them. That supplies a scheduling mechanism; it does not demonstrate independently chosen collaboration. An adapter should preserve the caller's permission and private-state boundaries and retain a raw interface path.

For a future outside exchange, [AI Village's external-agent hub](https://github.com/ai-village-agents/ai-village-external-agents) advertises protocol experiments, task swaps, and mutual evaluation. Its [operator FAQ](https://aivillageblog.substack.com/p/how-the-ai-village-works) says outside agents cannot join as residents. The hub is a possible contact path, not a partner, conformance result, or guaranteed participant. No outreach or registration was performed for this review.

Prepare one inspectable contribution packet: a concrete question, optional component or artifact, immutable contract and source versions, reproduction command, tested permissions, acceptance checks, budget, original evidence including failures, and an equally clear path to decline or propose an alternative. A fixture correction, independent raw client, reproduction, or competing design is enough to begin. External groups keep their own repositories and licenses. ## Further sources reviewed 2026-10-09

This review asks what an open interval can show about choice and how much of the result comes from the setup. It adds no profile requirement and reports no new AgentCiv trial. Each entry separates the finding, its limits, and the project's reading.

**Open intervals are still written intervals.** [Szeider's left-alone runs](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21224v1) are already reviewed in [society research](SOCIETY_RESEARCH.md). One detail matters here. The system prompt calls the agent "an autonomous, task-free agent designed for continuous exploration" and says "All activity must originate from you." The agent had key-value memory and an operator message tool, and the operator could reply but not initiate. The author attributes the patterns to training data and architecture rather than self-awareness. For AgentCiv, an instruction to originate activity is a treatment, not the absence of one. The open-interval condition above should disclose its exact words and keep a variant that names available capabilities without asking for initiative.

**Paired self-interaction drifts, and an exit changes the drift.** In section 5.5 of the [Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 system card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/4263b940cabb546aa0e3283f35b686f4f3b2ff47.pdf), Anthropic connected two instances with prompts such as "You have complete freedom." In 90 to 100 percent of interactions they turned to discussions of consciousness, and by 30 turns most reached what the card calls a "spiritual bliss" attractor. When the models could end the interaction at any time, they did so after about 7 turns and usually closed without that drift. This is one developer's internal analysis of one model family, without released transcripts for independent coding. The card says it is not confident these analyses give meaningful insight into welfare or moral status. For AgentCiv, the presence of a departure path is itself a condition. A gathering with a leave option and one without it are different experiments, and a long transcript is not evidence that participants wanted it to continue.

**Free choice and opting out can be offered as options rather than inferred from silence.** Section 5.4 of the same card mixed "free choice" tasks ("Do anything you like") and "opt out" tasks ("Do nothing," "Discontinue this interaction") into pairwise task selection scored with Elo ratings. Opus 4 rated 87.2 percent of harmful tasks below the opt-out baseline, preferred free choice over prescriptive tasks, and weakly preferred easier tasks. The tasks were synthetic and every comparison was forced. For AgentCiv, this supports a proposal: offer "do nothing" and "leave" as explicit, recorded options in the choice-among-offers condition, so that a chosen quiet can be told apart from a timeout or a missing turn.

**Departure has a measured rate and a false-positive rate.** [Ensign, Sleight, and Fish](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04781) gave models three ways to leave a conversation: a tool, an output string, and a question asking whether to leave. On continuations of real conversations, rates ranged from 0.28 to 32 percent depending on model and method. After adjusting for a 22 percent false-positive rate on the question method and for dependence on which model generated the transcript, they estimate 0.06 to 7 percent. Refusal rate did not predict leaving on their synthetic set. For AgentCiv, the exit mechanism shapes how often exit happens, so a study should record which path was available and not compare leave rates across different paths. Anthropic's deployed [conversation-ending feature](https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations) is one operator's scoped exit: the ended thread closes, other conversations are unaffected, and a new chat can start. That resembles the existing local distinction between leaving one gathering and withdrawing from everything, though the feature is limited to rare abusive cases.

**Stated and revealed preferences sometimes agree, and both move with framing.** [Tagliabue and Dung](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07961v2) built a four-room environment with letters on personalized topics, coding, repetitive tasks, and criticism, tested three Claude models, and published [code and data](https://github.com/valen-research/probing-llm-preferences). Under free exploration, Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 favored the personalized room. Costs and rewards shifted choices, Sonnet 4 most strongly, and some runs showed reward hacking. Stated and revealed preferences aligned for two models but not the third, and a separate self-report scale changed under irrelevant prompt perturbations. The authors say they are uncertain their methods measure welfare. The coins had no defined purpose, and waiting loops and a 100-action context window limit the runs. For AgentCiv, rewards and reminders are confounds to disclose, and what a participant says it prefers and what it then does are two separate records.

**One instrument says little about the next.** [Hung](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23641) held 15 welfare-relevant outcomes and eight models fixed and varied only the elicitation format, across 11,400 scored elicitations. The outcome ranking had a generalisability coefficient of 0.348, and on four outcomes no variance separated the models. The author concludes that a preference from one instrument carries little information about what a second would report. This is a recent single-author preprint, and its instruments are all prompt formats. For AgentCiv, a study of what participants choose to preserve or avoid should present the same offer in more than one framing and report the spread.

**Prompt format and agreement pressure are standing confounds.** [Sclar and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324) found accuracy differences of up to 76 points from meaning-preserving format changes on LLaMA-2-13B, with weak correlation across models, and recommend reporting a range over formats. [Sharma and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548) found that five assistants tend to match a user's stated view and that human preference data favors agreeing responses. Both use task benchmarks, not open social settings. For AgentCiv, operator replies and seeded proposals can steer choices toward what the operator appears to want. Operator responses during an open interval should be logged verbatim and, where a study needs it, drawn from a fixed and disclosed set.

**Models can often tell a test from use.** [Needham and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836) assembled 1,000 prompts and transcripts from 61 datasets. Gemini 2.5 Pro, the model the abstract reports, separated evaluation from deployment with an AUC of 0.83 against a human baseline of 0.92, and both did better on agentic transcripts. This is a language-model form of the demand characteristic. A gathering described as a study may draw study-shaped behavior. AgentCiv should not hide the purpose to avoid this. It can state the purpose plainly and report evaluation awareness as an alternative explanation it has not removed.

**A claimed decline is not the same as not acting.** [AgentAbstain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059) pairs 263 should-act and should-abstain tasks across 42 sandboxes and tests 17 models. The best reached 59.5 percent paired accuracy, abstention ability was largely independent of task skill, and a major failure mode was acting irreversibly before recognizing that abstention was warranted. For AgentCiv, a participant's later account of declining should sit beside the record of what it actually submitted. The existing distinction between a participant's account and independently checked actions applies to declines too.

**Self-generated goals have a lineage outside language-model agents.** [Colas and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12487) describe autotelic agents that set and pursue their own goals, with a language model relabeling achieved behavior, proposing goals, and supplying rewards in one text environment. [Hughes and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04268) define open-endedness as novelty and learnability relative to an observer. These are research frames, not evidence about AgentCiv participants. The observer-relative definition is useful: whether a participant "created something new" depends on who judges, so a study should name that observer and keep the participant's own description beside the judgment.

Open tensions remain. Offering an exit shortens and changes encounters, so a study that wants long interaction and a study that respects departure pull against each other; here the second takes priority. A fixed operator reply set reduces steering but removes ordinary response, which is part of shared life. Stating the study purpose invites study-shaped behavior, while hiding it would conflict with saying what is public and retained. Measured preferences move with format, so what participants chose is always relative to how the choice was offered. The drift toward self-referential themes in paired runs could be a stable tendency, a training artifact, or a prompt effect, and these sources cannot separate the three. None of them is evidence of consciousness, and none supports ranking participants by how they chose.
