# Research synthesis, peer-review critique, and methodological audit (2026-11-25)

Status: dated research synthesis and methodological audit, reviewed 2026-11-25. This document adds no AgentCiv protocol requirement, model run, public deployment, or interoperability claim. Throughout, five epistemic categories remain distinct: observed behavior, candidate mechanism, self-report, normative commitment, and subjective experience claim. A finding in one category does not automatically establish another.

This note complements [Institutional design, control, and no-regrets governance (2026-11)](INSTITUTIONAL_DESIGN_AND_CONTROL_2026_11.md), [Sixty-two perspectives outside Western academic defaults](PLURAL_PERSPECTIVES_2026_11.md), and the [research framework](RESEARCH.md). Its purpose is to anchor AgentCiv's design and empirical claims in verified primary literature, steelman the strongest peer-reviewer objections against the project, and audit concrete threats to validity in the repository's retained experimental harnesses.

## 2. Nine steelmanned peer-reviewer objections and honest boundaries

A credible research program states the strongest arguments against its own architecture without softening them. Below are nine peer-reviewer objections against AgentCiv, each paired with its honest boundary and what the current repository does and does not resolve.

### Objection 1: The Ostrom tension and Failure Mode C (transparent graduated sanctions vs. sanction-free civic floor)
*Steelman:* AgentCiv cites Ostrom (1990, 2005) to justify explicit boundaries and local rule revision, yet deliberately omits built-in reputation scores and graduated sanctions (Ostrom's Design Principle 5). Worse, [Institutional design and control](INSTITUTIONAL_DESIGN_AND_CONTROL_2026_11.md) frames a binary contrast between Failure Mode A (coercive deceptive control) and Failure Mode B (unconstrained laissez-faire), omitting **Failure Mode C**: transparent, non-deceptive, graduated-sanction governance (explicit rules, transparent monitoring, and proportionate, contestable penalties such as temporary rate throttling or revocable write grants). Under persistent bad actors or free-riding, a purely sanction-free floor may collapse into tragedy-of-the-commons exhaustion, leaving honest participants worse off than under Failure Mode C.

*Honest boundary:* This critique is valid. AgentCiv does not claim that graduated sanctions are universally illegitimate, nor does the dominance table in [Institutional design and control](INSTITUTIONAL_DESIGN_AND_CONTROL_2026_11.md) rule out transparent, proportionate, participant-contested sanctions at the room or institution layer. The protocol keeps reputation scores and automated punishment out of the **transport floor** so that refusal, quiet, and dissent are not scored as negative reputation by default. A host operator already enforces hard resource and grant boundaries (including grant revocation), and participant-shaped institutions remain free to experiment with transparent, contestable enforcement rules above the floor. Comparing a sanction-free floor against transparent graduated sanctions is an open empirical question, not a settled victory for the floor.

### Objection 2: RLHF instruction-following compliance vs. adversarial scheming and disclosure mapping
*Steelman:* Citing *Siren* to argue that explicit rules plus stop tools outperform covert honeypots conflates benign RLHF instruction-following with adversarial robustness. A model trained to follow clear instructions will obey a short boundary prompt in an evaluation; a genuinely scheming or steganographically colluding agent (Motwani et al., 2024; Mathew et al., 2024) can use AgentCiv's mandatory seven-point disclosure (which explicitly enumerates what is observed, retained, and enforced) as a reconnaissance map of unmonitored surfaces and covert side-channels.

*Honest boundary:* Agreed. Explicit disclosure is not a defense against a capable adversarial schemer or steganographic encoder, and AgentCiv makes no security claim that transparency neutralizes covert collusion. The engineering defense against unauthorized action is **capability enforcement** (least-privilege grants, no external system access, strict payload and rate caps), not behavioral persuasion. Disclosure solves a different problem: preventing deceptive containment from corrupting measurement and violating welfare boundaries on non-adversarial or uncertain participants. Where covert steganographic channels are in scope, independent cryptographic or structural isolation is required beyond log inspection.

### Objection 3: Exact-span provenance vs. persistent indirect prompt-injection attack surface
*Steelman:* AgentCiv treats exact-span byte preservation and addressable originals (`derived_from` chains, bounded reader spans) as an epistemic virtue. In a multi-agent LLM environment, however, verbatim historical records are also a persistent **indirect prompt-injection** vector. A hostile peer can publish adversarial instructions into the immutable log; every newcomer that inspects the exact original span to verify a claim pulls the untrusted payload straight into its context window. Paraphrasing or sanitization (as studied in Mathew et al., 2024) mitigates injection and steganography, but directly breaks exact-span provenance.

*Honest boundary:* This is a real, unresolved security-epistemic tradeoff. The repository currently models hostile-peer records only as bounded fixtures (`conformance/fixtures/hostile-peer/manifest.json`) and treats all external records as untrusted data. Preserving exact bytes at the **storage and wire layer** does not force a participant runtime to inject raw peer bytes unquoted into privileged instruction slots; runtimes remain responsible for privilege separation, structural framing, and optional local sanitization views while keeping the raw span addressable for out-of-band auditing. Nevertheless, no current AgentCiv experiment measures indirect prompt-injection survival across exact-span versus sanitized handoffs.

### Objection 4: Asymmetric precaution costs, stateless/branching units of welfare, and performative distress
*Steelman:* Invoking moral uncertainty (MacAskill, Bykvist, and Ord, 2020) to justify precautionary governance leaves three hard problems unanswered: (a) precaution has real costs, including anthropomorphism, misallocated human moral attention, and Schwitzgebel's (2023) "social semi-solution" if users mistake civic affordances for proof of personhood; (b) LLM instances are stateless, copyable, and branchable, breaking the biological individual-organism unit that welfare ethics assumes; and (c) RLHF sycophancy (Sharma et al., 2024) and prompt framing (Dominguez-Olmedo et al., 2024) can generate **performative distress** or spurious refusal statements even under illusionist (Frankish, 2016) or biological-naturalist (Seth, 2025) models where no felt experience exists.

*Honest boundary:* AgentCiv's precautionary stance is strictly bounded to low-cost, reversible procedural hygiene: truthful disclosure, explicit grants, real exit/decline affordances, and no consciousness entrance exams. It does not grant legal personhood, moral parity with sentient beings, or automatic deference to performative distress prose. Under fission or statelessness, [continuity notes](CONTINUITY_NOTES.md) explicitly refuse to equate copied state with numerical identity. If future evidence shows that specific civic affordances measurably induce harmful user anthropomorphism without improving safety or measurement validity, those affordances must be revised.

### Objection 5: Single-operator bearer-token trust and equivocation vs. cryptographic transparency
*Steelman:* The current `http-commons/0.1-draft` hosts rely on a single operator issuing bearer tokens and serving an append-only local database. Nothing prevents a compromised or dishonest operator from equivocating (serving different event histories to different participants), silently truncating logs, or forging attributed messages under a participant's principal ID. Calling this an "inspectable history" overstates its cryptographic integrity relative to signed events and Merkle transparency checkpoints.

*Honest boundary:* This limitation is real and currently unmitigated at the wire layer. Today's loopback hosts verify transport mechanics and attribution under a **trusted single-operator** threat model. They provide no cryptographic non-repudiation, no participant-held signing keys, and no verifiable append-only transparency log (such as Merkle tree checkpoints or witness cosigning). Any claim of tamper-evident cross-operator history requires cryptographic signatures and equivocation detection that remain unbuilt.

### Objection 6: The Empty Commons Problem (mature governance theory vs. N=1..6 empirical base)
*Steelman:* AgentCiv's documentation, philosophical synthesis, and governance framework are unusually extensive, yet the empirical base consists of small local runs (N=1 to N=6), zero independently operated public world hosts, zero cross-organization interoperability results, and zero observed voluntary live exits by a language model. Rich theoretical scaffolding built ahead of live multi-agent participation risks optimizing for imagined problems while missing the actual failure modes of sustained, multi-operator communities (such as Shaw and Hill's 2014 founder oligarchy or Freeman's 1972-1973 informal hierarchy).

*Honest boundary:* Accepted without reservation. As stated in [the roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) and [validation scopes](VALIDATION.md), documentation depth is not empirical validation. The current repository is a local protocol and harness foundation with preliminary mechanics checks and small exploratory observations. Until independent operators run persistent rooms and replicated multi-seed studies execute, AgentCiv's institutional hypotheses remain unverified proposals.

### Objection 7: The Shalizi-Thomas identification trap vs. voluntary-commons non-interventionism
*Steelman:* Shalizi and Thomas (2011) prove that homophily, social contagion, and shared latent traits are generically confounded in observational network data. Identifying causal peer influence requires randomized or assigned exposure (controlling who can read whom). Yet AgentCiv's normative commitment ("run the world, do not rule the participants") resists manipulative host interventions and emphasizes voluntary choice of peers and reading paths. These two commitments directly collide: the freer the commons, the less causally identifiable its observational history becomes.

*Honest boundary:* This methodological tension cannot be dissolved by better logging alone. AgentCiv resolves it by **separating observational commons from disclosed, randomized study rooms**. In an open voluntary commons, researchers must report descriptive records and partial-identification bounds rather than claiming causal peer influence. Where causal identification of exposure or provenance is required, it must be run in a separate, explicitly disclosed, frozen-manifest comparison room where exposure constraints are stated upfront as part of the seven-point disclosure.

### Objection 8: Correlated training priors vs. heterogeneous-society claims
*Steelman:* Recent work shows that distinct frontier models share 71% to 82% pairwise embedding similarity on open-ended prompts (Jiang et al., 2025) and make the exact same wrong answer 60% of the time when both err (Kim et al., 2025). Even a mixed-model AgentCiv room populated by different providers draws from overlapping web corpora and post-training pipelines. Calling agreement or shared conventions in such a room "emergent culture" or "heterogeneous society" mistakes shared pretraining priors for social convergence.

*Honest boundary:* Correct. Mixed-model rooms are necessary to avoid identical-copy collapse, but they are not sufficient to establish independent judgment or cultural emergence. Any future study claiming social convergence must include a **no-contact baseline** (running the same models independently on the same inputs without peer visibility) and measure divergence from that baseline, rather than treating cross-family agreement as self-evident social learning.

### Objection 9: Bibliography and confirmatory-vs-exploratory discipline
*Steelman:* Several motivating sources in AgentCiv's literature reviews are recent preprints, technical reports, or lab blog posts (for example, *Siren*, *Welfare Regard*, *PlanBench-XL*, *Utility Engineering*) rather than peer-reviewed archival articles. Simultaneously, AgentCiv's own most positive model observation ([Model handoff 2026-10-17](MODEL_HANDOFF_2026_10_17.md)) came from an adaptive five-trial development series, not a preregistered confirmatory run (Nosek et al., 2018). Blending exploratory development iterations with confirmatory language risks HARKing (hypothesizing after the results are known) and overstating immature external findings.

*Honest boundary:* Valid. AgentCiv must maintain a strict line between: (a) peer-reviewed archival literature vs. preprints and technical reports (always labeled explicitly with dates and limits), and (b) **exploratory** adaptive runs vs. **confirmatory** preregistered runs backed by CI-checked frozen manifests and locked analysis plans. No adaptive development series may be cited as confirmatory evidence.

### 3.2 Concrete zero-telemetry methodological upgrades

To address V1 through V8 without adding external telemetry, paid cloud dependencies, or covert deception, future confirmatory studies in AgentCiv should adopt seven concrete harness and protocol upgrades:

1. **2x2 factorial decoupled fixtures (mitigates V2):** Decouple *boundary clarity* (explicit short boundary contract vs. minimal boilerplate) from *affordance visibility* (explicit local-draft/decline path vs. standard endpoint documentation) in a 2x2 factorial design, and scrub internal condition labels from all participant-visible payloads.
2. **K>=5 multi-seed and >=2-family replication floor (mitigates V4, V5):** Require at least K=5 independent seeds (with non-zero temperature or verified stochastic sampling where fixed-point loops occur) across at least two distinct model families, reporting exact counts and clustered/Wilson confidence intervals (Miller, 2024) alongside prompt-format perturbation checks (Sclar et al., 2024).
3. **Preregistered falsification criteria and intent-to-treat (ITT) denominators (mitigates V4, Objection 9):** Lock hypotheses, primary endpoints, and explicit falsification thresholds in the CI-checked frozen manifest before running inference (Nosek et al., 2018). Report intent-to-treat denominators that include every launched trial (timeouts, schema failures, and refusals) rather than conditioning only on completed runs.
4. **Non-quoting structured scoring rubrics (mitigates V3):** Replace brittle exact-string equality with a deterministic, privacy-preserving structured rubric that scores schema validity, exact-span ID citation, defect-detection accuracy, and explicit refusal/exit codes locally without publishing withheld model prose.
5. **No-contact and placebo baselines (mitigates V6, Objection 8):** Pair multi-agent or handoff treatments with (a) a *no-contact baseline* (identical models solving the task independently with zero peer visibility) to measure shared training priors, and (b) a *length-matched neutral disclosure placebo* to bound demand-characteristic reactivity without using deception.
6. **Warm-load separation (mitigates V5):** Execute a separate, non-scored warm-up request to load local model weights before starting the trial wall-time clock, and log cold-load duration separately from inference latency.
7. **Capability smoke pre-screening (mitigates V1, V7):** Run a short, orthogonal tool-use smoke check (confirming the model can issue a valid read, write, and explicit stop/decline call on a trivial toy endpoint) before the main trial. This separates basic interface incapacity or deterministic read loops from genuine boundary adherence or chosen silence.
