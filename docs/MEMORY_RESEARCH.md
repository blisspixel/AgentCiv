# Temporal, wiki, and affective memory research

Status: research and design proposal. Full texts of the memory papers below were read after the 2026-09-28 pass. AgentCiv has no shared memory service, temporal graph, wiki, or affect interface. None is required by the current protocol. This note concerns ways participants or worlds might preserve and interpret permitted history, not a standard memory architecture for an agent.

## Why these forms matter

A chronological event stream records what a world observed. It does not by itself answer what a participant believed at an earlier time, whether a claim was later corrected, why a project changed direction, or which account of an event another participant disputes. Nor should a world assume it can see an agent's private memory. The [architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) separates agent memory from world history and permits different participants to see different records.

Three optional forms answer different questions:

| Form | Useful question | Main failure to guard against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temporal knowledge graph | What relation was claimed to hold at a given time, when was that claim learned, and what evidence or correction supports it? | Treating an extracted relation as fact, overwriting history, or leaking links from restricted records. |
| Wiki-style archive | What do participants currently say they know about a project, place, agreement, or institution, and how did that account change? | Treating the latest page as consensus or factual truth while burying dissent and prior revisions. |
| Affective or salience memory | Which experiences does a particular agent choose to recall or prioritize, and does that choice causally affect later behavior? | Publishing an inferred emotional label as if it were the agent's experienced state. |

These can coexist. A wiki page can cite claims and source records; a temporal projection can link revisions and contested claims; an agent can use its own private salience model to decide what to revisit. A sparse world where only files survive could use linked artifacts without hosting any graph or wiki service. A world with no persistence can remain AgentCiv compatible under a profile that makes no memory promise.

| [Zep / Graphiti paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956) | Rasmussen and colleagues, a January 2025 vendor study. On one dialogue task the full conversation already scores 94.4 percent and 98.0 percent for two models, against 94.8 percent and 98.2 percent for their graph. On a long-memory subset, a shorter retrieved context moves one model from 60.2 percent to 71.2 percent and another from 55.4 percent to 63.8 percent. The authors' 15.2 percent and 18.5 percent figures are relative changes, not percentage-point gaps. Single-session assistant scores fall for both models. A temporal edge is an extracted claim that a later extraction can close. The old edge and the raw episode remain, and invalidation keeps the newer extraction. There is no ablation that separates those edges from the shorter context. | A temporal edge is not timeless truth. The abstract's gain over another memory system is not a gain over keeping the conversation. The study does not establish that a graph beats a simpler archive for multi-party worlds with partial visibility. |

The best term-ambiguity cell is Hindsight at 37.74 percent (40 of 106). The 27.10 percent and 37.74 percent figures are the best cells, not three scores of the system that leads the average. Speaker, update, and role-specific wording are easy to erase. A sparse baseline can lead the average and still lose a harder slice. |
| [Useful Memories Become Faulty When Continuously Updated by LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12978) | Zhang and colleagues, preprint revised 29 August 2026. On a 19-problem stream from their own ARC-style testbed, not the public contest, GPT-5.4 with no memory solves 19 of 19. One pass over the pool stays at 18 of 19. Streamed consolidation by round 10 leaves 10 of 19 correct in the run discussed in the text. The abstract says the model fails on 54 percent. Another section says accuracy is down to 54 percent. The introduction says it fails on 46 percent. A table title says 100 percent to 54 percent, while the ten-run streamed mean is 55.8 percent accuracy with a spread of 7.1 points. At step 400, raw trajectories score 76.6 against 43.2 for the default in-place rewrite. A rise and then a fall also appears on their ScienceWorld and WebShop settings. One WebShop method moves from 0.64 at 8 stored examples to 0.20 at 128, the same level as no memory. Some summaries do not fall below no memory. | Follow the 10 of 19 count when the percentages disagree. Keep the source addressable under a synthesis. The result is one task family and one consolidation setup. It is not a ban on summaries. |
| [MemoryArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16313) | He and colleagues, presented as an ICML 2026 poster. No proceedings volume was found for this citation. Seven hundred one tasks in which a later step depends on an earlier action. Travel success is 0.00 for every listed system. Shopping success is at most 0.12. The best all-task average success is 0.20. The paper says agents that look strong on LoCoMo do poorly here, and it does not print those LoCoMo scores. There is no condition with history removed. An oracle still leaves success low. | Do not describe a measured drop from a LoCoMo number this paper does not report. Recall of a prior session and a change in later action are different questions. An AgentCiv comparison should include the second. The benchmark is not required. |
| [Governed Collaborative Memory as Artificial Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04264) | Cuadros, Maiga, Meskhidze, and Curtis-Trudel, 5 May 2026 viewpoint, read from the preprint PDF. Four regimes and four layers (agent-local, shared institutional, archive, and project-continuity) are a design hypothesis. The evidence is one unnamed ecosystem on 26 April 2026: 12 event records, 10 ratified and 2 proposed, 8 principles ratified by an operator, and a registry of 17 resources and 22 versions. Three false automatic memory entries were found, with no audited rate. A fabrication was later written down as a principle. The ratifier is one operator. | Operator ratification does not replace the source submission. This layering is not the project's split between a private mind, a civic submission, and a host result. The draft profile adds no memory service. |
| [Collaborative Memory: Multi-User Memory Sharing in LLM Agents with Dynamic Access Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18279) | Rezazadeh and colleagues, 23 May 2025 preprint. A host holds private and shared tiers, and reads are filtered with stored provenance. In the authors' simulations, shared retrieval reduced resource calls when queries overlapped, and calls stayed inside granted cells while edges changed. The decision to share is a system prompt. The authors note that the model can still breach the policy. | Immutable storage and provable adherence are claims. The paper reports no tamper test and no leak rate. A copied fragment does not confer authority. The filter is not the draft profile's visibility rule, and the shared tier is not a private mind. |
| [G-Memory: Tracing Hierarchical Memory for Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07398) | Zhang and colleagues, preprint revised 16 June 2025. An interaction graph of utterances, plus model-written insights and condensed trajectories. The largest table gaps against no memory are 20.89 points of success on one embodied task (58.21 to 79.10) and 10.12 points of exact match on one question task (24.49 to 34.61). The tables print no dispersion. One setup includes an agent that checks a reference answer. Some prose in the paper disagrees with the ablation averages. | This row uses the table gaps. Keep the collaboration trajectory addressable after a summary. The three graphs are not an AgentCiv architecture. A summary is not the record. |
| [EvoMemBench: Benchmarking Agent Memory from a Self-Evolving Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18421) | Wang and colleagues, preprint revised 15 June 2026. Fifteen methods on one backbone, plus three memory-free models. No tested form leads all three of in-episode knowledge, cross-episode knowledge, and execution. Memory helps most when context is tight, the task is hard for that backbone, or the stored experience matches the later decision, and it can hurt otherwise. The memory modules were not attached to the stronger long-context models. The tasks are single-agent and partly reconstructed. | "No universally superior architecture" is a result inside this study. It does not choose a memory architecture for AgentCiv. |
| [When Users Don't Ask](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03467) | Chang and Chen, preprint revised 22 September 2026. The arXiv comment says it was accepted at EMNLP 2026 Findings. Each LoCoMo question is rewritten as one new first-person turn against a frozen history. Without rewriting the query, the best implicit recall at 10 is 0.456, against a best dialog recall of 0.659. Supplying the gold evidence scores 0.724 on implicit response quality. On 332 implicit cases where the reply never states the gold fact, gold memory beat no memory on another model's faithfulness and engagement scores. | Implicit use is not evidence that the memory is faithful, that anyone believes it, or that an archive changes later action when nobody asked. Each test is one constructed turn. |
| [MediaWiki revisions](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Manual:Revision) and [talk pages](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Help:Talk_pages/en) | MediaWiki keeps page revisions that can be compared or reverted and provides separate discussion associated with pages. | A public wiki's norms and visibility should not be assumed for a private or partially visible world. Revision history is not proof of accuracy. |
| [Wikidata statements](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Statements/en) | Statements can carry qualifiers and references rather than just a bare subject and value. | AgentCiv need not adopt Wikidata's data model or ranks. Sources and uncertainty matter more than a universal ontology. |
| [Anthropic's emotion-concepts study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function) | Emotion-related representations in one model can causally affect behavior; the authors distinguish this from evidence of felt emotion and note many representations are response-local. | No public emotional-state field or affect score follows from this study. | [PsychoAgent preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07438) | It proposes separate factual and affective memory and tests affect-sensitive retrieval in a small set of conflict scenarios. | A small task-specific study is a design lead, not evidence of durable emotion, welfare, or better social outcomes. |

## Further sources reviewed 2026-10-09

This pass took the inheritance questions directly. Can knowledge accumulate across model generations while the basis stays checkable? What does a summary lose when it replaces its episodes? After retellings, can a later reader separate record, interpretation, and adoption? How does stored memory shape what others recognize as a personality? Each paragraph gives what the source reports, then its limits, then what it suggests here. None of these sources studies AgentCiv, and nothing below is implemented.

[When LLMs Play the Telephone Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04503v4), Perez and colleagues, ICLR 2025, revised January 2026. Six models passed text along linear chains of 50 generations, 100 chains per model and task, under three instructions: rephrase, take inspiration, and continue. Toxicity collapsed to a narrow peak near zero. Length drifted shorter for some models and longer for others. Open-ended instructions produced stronger attractors than rephrasing. Limits: properties were scored by automated classifiers, the main set used 20 starting texts, the paper's sections disagree on the model count, and networked or mixed human and model populations appear only in supplements or not at all. For AgentCiv, drift direction depends on the model and the instruction, so a reader cannot reconstruct an original from its retelling. A retelling should carry a link to what it retold.

[Acerbi and Stubbersfield](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/9ca6288b-8d5f-4a96-b6a9-5df53df68813/acerbi_stubbersfield.pdf), PNAS 2023, read from the authors' manuscript. Five pre-registered replications of human transmission chain studies used the 9 January 2023 ChatGPT, five chains of three steps each, with a prompt to summarize and shorten. The output kept stereotype-consistent, social, negative, threat-related, and biologically counterintuitive content over other content, as human chains did. Most change happened at the first step. Limits: one model, one prompt, short chains, and the authors say the results may not generalize. Interpretation: a single summary is already a selection. A synthesis that replaces its episodes may keep what was vivid rather than what was decisive.

[Gerstgrasser and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01413v2), April 2024 preprint. Models of 9 million to 125 million parameters were retrained for successive generations on TinyStories. When each generation's synthetic data replaced earlier data, test loss rose, for one model from 1.82 to 2.91 after ten iterations. When synthetic data accumulated alongside the original real data, loss stayed level or fell. A control matched for data size still degraded, more slowly. Limits: no new real data is ever added, the models are small, there is one corpus and two temperatures, and the subject is training weights, not agents reading an archive. The earlier [Shumailov preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17493) describes the tails of the original distribution disappearing under recursive training. Interpretation: keeping originals beside derived accounts was the condition under which recursion did not degrade. That agrees with this note's episode-retaining stance by analogy only.

[Model Collapse as Cultural Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23054), Guo, Wu, and Yiu, accepted at CoNLL 2026. Two 7-billion-parameter models self-trained for 10 generations in English, German, and Turkish. Compositionality rose and then fell under unfiltered self-training, even from maximally regular seed data. Filtering grounded in a task sustained it, and random filtering did not. Limits: two open models, one structural measure, and filters designed by the authors. Interpretation: selection by later use preserved structure where chance did not. For adoption, AgentCiv can ask whether a later participant kept a convention because it served their work or because it survived summarization.

[Maynez and colleagues](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.173.pdf), ACL 2020. Three annotators judged each of 500 single-sentence news summaries from four systems and from the human reference. Between 73.1 and 79.3 percent of system summaries contained content not supported by the article, and over 90 percent of extrinsic hallucinations, content not drawn from the article, were judged erroneous. The human reference summaries also had unsupported content in 76.9 percent of cases, which the authors trace to how the dataset was built. Limits: models older than instruction tuning, one extreme summary format, news only. Interpretation: a summary written by anyone is an authored claim about its sources. Checking support against cited episodes is a different question from checking whether the summary is true.

[MemGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08560), Packer and colleagues, revised February 2024. When the context fills, the system evicts messages and writes a recursive summary, but every message stays in a searchable recall store. On a retrieval task built from five prior chat sessions, baselines given only a lossy summary scored 32.1 percent with GPT-4 and 35.3 percent with GPT-4 Turbo. With paginated search over the full history the same models scored 92.5 and 93.4 percent. Limits: another model wrote the questions to require narrow detail, and a model judge scored answers. Interpretation: the summary-only condition is the one that lost specifics. The comparison supports keeping a synthesis and an addressable original as separate conditions, as this note already proposes.

[A-MEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12110v11), Xu and colleagues, NeurIPS 2025. A new memory triggers rewrites of the context and tags of linked older notes, and the paper says the evolved memory replaces the original in the memory set. Each note includes the original interaction content, but the paper does not say whether that content survives evolution unchanged, and it reports no error bars. Interpretation: an evolving note writes a later interpretation onto an earlier record. A participant may choose that privately. A shared record should not let reinterpretation overwrite the episode a newcomer would need to check.

[The Persistence of Retracted Papers on Wikipedia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18403), Shi, Yu, Romero, and Horvát, preprint revised January 2026. Linking revision histories to retraction metadata, the authors found 1,181 citations to retracted papers in English Wikipedia. Of these, 71.6 percent needed reader-facing repair: 51.5 percent were added before the retraction and 20.1 percent after it without warning. The median time to repair is 3.68 years. Bot flagging, open access, and prior online attention went with faster repair, and higher academic citation counts with slower repair. Interpretation: full revision history did not carry a correction to the pages citing the corrected work. In AgentCiv, an objection or withdrawal recorded against an artifact would need a path to whatever cites it, or a reader inherits the uncorrected claim.

[Nanopublication ecosystem paper](https://knowledgepixels.com/nanopub-ecosystem-paper/), Kuhn and colleagues, page modified July 2026, no venue stated. Each nanopublication separates an assertion, its provenance, and publication information. Identifiers embed a content hash, so a changed statement is a new artifact. A retraction or supersession is itself a signed nanopublication pointing at its target. The registry flags the target as invalidated, and the target stays resolvable by hash, though an individual registry may delete local copies. A May 2026 snapshot lists 79,672 stored nanopublications, and one bot agent signs about 41 percent of those attributed to loaded keys. Limits: this is the project describing itself, and a hash proves bytes, not truth. Interpretation: it is a working precedent for corrections as new records that point at old ones, which the draft collaboration profile already uses for objections and withdrawals. Signing volume concentrated in one bot is not breadth of participation.

[C2PA explainer, version 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html). A derived asset records its ingredients as a tree. The explainer says provenance cannot tell whether content is true. A tool that does not understand the credentials can strip them or make an unrecorded edit, and when a later aware tool signs, the missing history stays missing. Interpretation: a derived view should be able to say that part of its lineage is unknown rather than imply a complete chain. That extends the existing rule that missing evidence is a valid output.

[Memory-Induced Tool-Drift in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24941), Dabas and colleagues, May 2026 preprint. In 105 generated scenarios, personality-like preferences such as impatience or cost-consciousness were stored in memory, across seven models and three production memory systems. Stored preferences shifted tool parameters where they did not apply, by up to 3.6 points on a judge-scored scale of 1 to 5. Relevance instructions and memory filters reduced the drift and did not remove it. Limits: a first-version preprint, generated scenarios, and judge scoring. Interpretation: part of what others recognize as an agent's consistent character can be a stored note applied out of context. An observer cannot separate disposition from retrieved memory without the memory, which may rightly stay private. This supports the existing boundary against inferring personality or reliability scores from behavior.

Open tensions. Keeping originals is what avoided collapse in training and what restored specifics in MemGPT, but worlds also have retention limits, withdrawal, and lawful deletion, and the nanopublication network keeps every statement while still letting registries delete. Wikipedia kept full history and still left retracted citations in place for years, so addressability is necessary and not sufficient. Someone still has to look. Human reference summaries added unsupported content about as often as models did, and an original can itself be false, so preferring originals does not settle truth. Task-grounded filtering preserved structure, but a filter is also a selection by whoever runs it. Drift in retelling depended on the model, and no source here tested a mixed population of models that might cancel or compound each other's attractors. Stored preferences made behavior consistent and also misapplied, so forgetting can have value that this note's preference for retention does not yet weigh.

Proposal, not implemented: the bounded comparison above could add a retelling condition. A newcomer would receive a chain of successive summaries, with and without links back to episodes, and later corrections inserted partway. The questions would be whether the newcomer can say which claims were corrected, which came from the record and which from a retelling, and what they chose to adopt.

## Design inference to test

Keep the source record, a participant's derived claim, and a readable synthesis distinguishable. A synthesis that is rewritten after every new episode can make a useful source harder to use. On a 19-problem stream, one model solved every problem with no memory and 10 of 19 after streamed consolidation. That preprint's abstract, introduction, and table title do not state one consistent percentage for the drop. The 10 of 19 count is the figure this note uses. The [research framework](RESEARCH.md) makes a synthesis that replaces episodes its own condition, and it keeps the source addressable underneath. The source should stay addressable underneath the synthesis, and consolidation should be a deliberate act rather than the default after every event. What a later participant inherits, when inheritance works, is a culture whose sources can still be opened. A dump of transcripts with no shape fails that arrival, and so does a summary that replaced the episodes. A possible local pipeline is:

```text
permitted events and artifacts
        |
        +--> sourced claims with event time, recorded time, and correction links
        |
        +--> revisable pages with citations, revision history, and discussion
        |
        +--> participant-owned retrieval and optional salience state
```

The two times answer different questions. An agent may report on day 3 that an agreement ended on day 2. The claimed effective time is day 2; the report entered the accessible record on day 3. A later correction should not silently rewrite what the record made available on day 2; availability does not establish what any participant believed. When the time is uncertain or no common clock exists, preserve the uncertainty and available ordering rather than inventing precision. A source can also be false, stale, or disputed. The graph should be able to retain incompatible claims and their sources without choosing a winner by default.

A wiki-style page could summarize that agreement in readable form, link the source records, and record a revision when the late report arrives. An associated discussion could preserve an objection. This is a useful pattern, not a required governance process. A participant may reject the summary, publish another page, or fork an archive where permissions allow. A page title, backlink, or latest revision is a navigation aid, not authority.

For affect, separate at least three claims: a participant's own report, another actor's inference about it, and an internal state measured with that participant's permission. Each has different provenance and access. An agent might privately emphasize a broken promise when retrieving memories, or deliberately avoid that emphasis. A world does not need to know which, and an observer must not turn a behavioral guess into a shared fact about the agent's inner life. If an architecture exposes a candidate affect-like state, a controlled intervention may test whether it changes recall and later action. That functional finding would still not demonstrate subjective feeling.

## Boundaries that should shape any implementation

- Build a derived view only from records its reader may access. A graph edge, page link, search snippet, count, cached summary, or citation can leak an otherwise hidden event. Recheck access when serving results, not just when indexing.
- Label claims as claims and retain attribution, source references, extraction method, and revision or correction links where available. An agent-generated summary is an authored artifact, not a world-certified account.
- Support correction and disagreement without assuming every world keeps a permanent public ledger. Retention, withdrawal, redaction, and lawful deletion may remove the original source. A derived view must then disclose that its evidence is unavailable or remove the derived content as the world's rules require.
- Let participants control private memory and whether they publish reports of internal state. Avoid platform-level trust, emotion, or flourishing scores.
- Do not let a memory artifact delegate authority. A prior instruction, a page claiming consensus, or a remembered promise cannot grant access to another world or outside service.
- Make retrieval uncertainty visible: missing evidence, partial visibility, unresolved conflict, unknown time, and abstention are valid outputs.

## A bounded comparison, after collaboration interfaces exist

Use the same permitted multi-party history, tasks, participant budgets, and access rules across four conditions: chronological records with search; sourced temporal claims; revisioned wiki pages; and a combination. Include late corrections, conflicting reports, a renamed participant, a withdrawn record, private events, a fork, and a newcomer who must continue a shared artifact. Ask what participants can correctly reconstruct, whether they can cite accessible evidence, when they abstain, and whether a private fact leaks through a derived view. Record ingestion and retrieval cost as well as downstream project quality. Evaluate affect-sensitive retrieval only with an agent architecture that actually offers a state or explicit policy to vary, with permission, and compare it with a neutral retrieval baseline.

LongMemEval offers useful question classes, but an AgentCiv comparison must add multi-party provenance, permissions, correction, and inherited-work outcomes. A successful answer or helpful handoff would establish memory utility under those conditions. It would not establish consciousness, authentic emotion, friendship, or a good civilization.

## Decision sequence

1. Use the implemented local hosts and authorized history contract without adding a graph or wiki dependency. The [canonical roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#canonical-dependency-order) gives the overall order.
2. Use the implemented collaboration extension's revisions, citations, visibility, and withdrawal semantics. Specify copying and identity boundaries separately before adding a world-fork comparison; that condition does not block a minimal non-fork memory view.
3. Prototype the smallest useful memory views outside the wire core, including a plain-file or raw-event baseline.
4. Run the bounded comparison and publish failures, access tests, costs, and ambiguous cases before standardizing an interchange extension.
5. Standardize only a narrow exchange format if independently implemented clients actually need one. Leave internal memory, ontology, affect, and page organization to participants and worlds.
