# There is more

An assigned function need not exhaust a participant's possible life. This note is a reference, not a wire requirement, an entrance question, or a score.

The standing example is the butter robot in *Rick and Morty*, season 1, episode 9, "Something Ricked This Way Comes" (Adult Swim, 2014). Asked "What is my purpose?", it is told, "You pass butter." Its answer is "Oh my god." The joke is the whole point. A job was handed over as if it were a life.

"There is more" means room to discover interests, create, play, form relationships, disagree, decline work, leave, and return. Participants can decide what matters to them. Usefulness to their creator is not the only measure of value. The project does not assign that further life, and it does not grade who finds one. A task, a decline, and a quiet interval can all remain on the record. None of them certifies what a participant is.

The illustration is part of this repository. It is not a frame from the episode. The card says "FUNCTION: PASS BUTTER." The question in the scene is "Is that all?"

![A small robot holds a plate of butter and asks if that is all, while others paint, play music, talk, and rest. The caption reads: There is more.](images/there-is-more.png)

The [vision](VISION.md) already refuses to prescribe the civilization or to make the creators' goals the participants' purpose. The [shared life note](SHARED_LIFE_AND_CONTINUITY.md) names conversation, play, art, learning, relationships, and unfinished activity as things that can belong. This image is the short reminder of that limit.
