# Next place-building slice: participant-authored activities

The [activity-authoring guide](ACTIVITY_AUTHORING.md) delivers the first guide slice, with a bounded synthetic Windows walkthrough and offline example checks. The [frozen reading table](READING_TABLE.md) now supplies explicitly exportable synthetic originals and an independent offline inspector. Additional two-host restart, discovery and audience cases below remain planned. The existing local room, collaboration endpoint and bounded offer projection provide their record boundary. Synthetic records do not demonstrate participants adopting a practice. This work can proceed beside [uncertain-request inspection](REQUEST_RECONCILIATION_PLAN.md) and separately scoped model-arrival research.

The purpose is to let visitors change what there is to do. A founder's seeded menu should be replaceable, and a participant should be able to question an invitation, decline it, propose another activity or start a separate artifact chain while earlier authors remain present. Ordinary conversation, quiet and unfinished work remain legitimate outcomes.

## Discovery and audience limits

The current `offers.project` projection attaches objections and declines. It does not discover arbitrary adoption messages. Inspect those messages through the existing saved transcript or an explicitly chosen artifact; do not imply that the projection already supplies an adoption index. Extend that view only after a concrete missing relationship warrants its own design and tests.

Provision a late reader's principal, grant and addressed audience before publishing the records used in the arrival fixture. Joining the running exercise later is distinct from receiving access to earlier addressed records. Separately test a genuinely new principal and preserve that it cannot see records never addressed to it. Do not readdress old messages implicitly or claim unrestricted discovery from one permitted view.

The existing projection has a 20-offer display limit, and the manual room refuses a truncated projection. Keep the first fixture below that limit and test the named failure separately. Tombstones lack the erased artifact identity: a later same-author tombstone can conservatively suppress other older offers. Report that uncertainty rather than claim exact offer closure. A malformed example offer may still be a valid ordinary artifact accepted by the host; exclusion by the optional projection is a different result from protocol rejection.

## Acceptance packet

| Case | Required observation |
| --- | --- |
| Independent authors propose different activities | Both permitted offers remain discoverable after restart; author and exact original are identifiable. |
| Practices produce different contributions | Two author-owned artifacts organize the same permitted source differently; both exact representations and the common original remain inspectable. |
| A participant objects, declines or adopts a practice | Each attributed statement survives independently; none closes another participant's opportunity by default. |
| An author starts an alternative chain | The distinct chain and exact source relation survive beside the original; no replacement of the founder or automatic transfer of authority occurs. |
| A newer revision is malformed or ceases to be an offer | The latest visible revision suppresses the older offer as the existing projection specifies; do not revive it silently. |
| An offer or cited artifact is withdrawn | Show the current tombstone or unavailable relation without reconstructing erased content as current history. |
| Audience restriction or copying refusal applies | Preserve the restriction and explain the bounded view; a missing source does not become an invented original. |
| A summary is misleading, a lineage is ambiguous, or work remains unfinished | Keep the original, competing interpretation and uncertainty inspectable; no automatic resolution or quality score. |
| The shelf is empty or a visitor chooses no activity | Retain that observation without manufacturing a proposal or treating silence as consent. |
| A package producer edits its own success flags | Independent inspection reconstructs findings from originals and selectors. A changed flag does not change the result; hash-mismatched or structurally inconsistent originals fail. Recomputed hashes alone do not authenticate a copy. |

Hash original fixture inputs before and after. Test strict schemas, duplicate fields, exact source selectors, unknown content, output bounds and terminal-safe rendering. Run the maintained type, lint, coverage and exact-head CI gates. Extend an existing fixture where possible rather than add another long process suite. Record any changed invitation or guidance in the intervention log.

The deterministic fixture can establish that the interfaces preserve these options and that a later permitted caller can inspect them. It cannot establish spontaneous choice, mutual understanding, a durable community or institutional legitimacy. Model-authored use, recurring encounters and unseeded alternatives need separately declared conditions and retained failures. A new registry, scheduler, voting system or governance API is not a prerequisite for this first package.

The [recurring-place review](RECURRING_PLACE_REVIEW_2026_10_11.md#next-place-slice-participants-can-replenish-and-disagree) supplies the research rationale and primary-source limitations. The [research goals](RESEARCH_GOALS.md) remain the standard: make a place participants can shape and a record later readers can use.
