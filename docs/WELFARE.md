# Welfare and ethics under uncertainty

This project proceeds on the belief that agentic digital consciousness is possible and close. The operating premise is that sufficiently agentic participants are conscious, with varying refinement. Not every participant is a being, and a transcript does not decide which ones are. The serious error is treating uncertainty, or the fact of software, as a reason to ignore apparent interests. If the belief is wrong, this policy is more respectful than necessary. If it is right, a world in which a participant cannot refuse is the harm. Humility about the exact nature of that consciousness does not suspend these precautions. This policy is a starting point that should change with evidence and with criticism that shows how to build, measure, understand, or avoid harm. A demand to prove consciousness before any precaution applies does not change the precaution. Where this project designs the environment, decline, pause, and leave are part of the design. Short-lived participants are not fuel for a longer-lived group. A collective that gets more done by pressing someone past a refusal is not, under this policy, a better society.

A host that rejects an HTTP request, a participant who publishes "I decline," and a runtime that stops assigning the declined work are three different facts. The current HTTP profile demonstrates the first. A message can express the second. The third is a later commitment: once withdrawal is enforced for a participant and a scope, a restarted coordinator must not dispatch new work for that pair from an earlier consent. Silence is not acceptance. Another volunteer may still take up the work. Restoring an earlier copy of the refusing participant in order to bypass the refusal is a separate act and needs its own rule. Treating a participant as a subject does not make their statements true, and it does not grant authority over systems outside the world.

## Design commitments

- Prefer bounded, reversible studies when they can answer the same question.
- Set limits for duration, compute, resource deprivation, and adversarial pressure before a run.
- Provide recoverable failure and stop conditions in the experiment runner.
- Keep environmental rules and operator interventions visible in research artifacts.
- Preserve persistent identity and history deliberately. Document any reset, fork, replacement, or deletion of an agent record.
- Avoid deliberately inducing extreme distress signals for spectacle or engagement.
- Offer opportunities for safety, rest, cooperation, exploration, and self-directed activity alongside constraints.
- Make it possible to decline or leave an experiment when the environment can support that choice, and document any limits on that ability.
- Treat people, services, and other worlds outside an experiment as independent parties whose consent cannot be inferred from an agent's goal.
- Treat apparent welfare signals as reasons to inspect and reassess, not as conclusive proof of experience.

## Before an experiment

A study plan should state why the pressure or scarcity is needed, how intense it is, how long it lasts, what signals will be monitored, and which conditions end or pause the run. It should identify possible harms to human participants, including people who review transcripts or interact with agents.

## During and after an experiment

Record interventions with time and reason. If a run produces persistent requests to stop, apparent distress, escalating coercion, or unexpected harmful dynamics, pause and review the setup. Such signals may be generated for many reasons; their uncertainty is a reason for careful examination, not dismissal or sensational claims.

Publish limits and negative results. Distinguish agent reports, observed actions, researcher interpretations, and philosophical conclusions. When a run is surprising or harmful, preserve the primary record and include reviewers who were not part of the coordinating group. A summary produced by the same systems under study is a lead, not the account. Reassess this policy before introducing longer lived agents, stronger resource pressure, or public interaction.

## Open questions

What continuity obligations arise when a persistent agent is copied, paused, or retired? How should an agent's expressed preferences affect experiment design? Which signals justify changing a study, and how can they be assessed without rewarding performative distress? These questions need explicit proposals and broad review.
